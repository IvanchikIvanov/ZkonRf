Section 1. Core legal act.
Belarus crypto regulation is centered on Presidential Decree No. 8 of 21 December 2017 on the Development of the Digital Economy and the High Technologies Park regime. The decree introduced a legal framework for digital signs, commonly called tokens, and related blockchain activities.

Section 2. Token operations.
The Belarus regime covers creation, placement, storage, alienation, exchange, and other operations with tokens, especially through High Technologies Park residents and approved crypto-platform or exchange operators. A practical answer should check whether the operator is a HTP resident and whether the specific activity is authorized.

Section 3. Individuals and business activity.
Questions from individuals should distinguish personal acquisition, holding, mining, sale, exchange, investment, and professional service activity. Professional or repeated activity, custody, brokerage, platform, bank-like, or exchange services may require a regulated operator and specific internal rules.

Section 4. Tax and compliance.
Belarus crypto questions often include tax benefits, accounting treatment, token income, foreign platforms, AML/KYC controls, and cross-border payment routes. Always verify the current tax period and whether special rules have been extended or amended.

Section 5. Consumer and fraud issues.
For fraud, blocked withdrawals, fake investment projects, lost wallets, or token-sale disputes, combine this source with criminal, civil, consumer, banking, personal-data, and appeals materials. Key facts are operator status, user agreement, advertisements, wallet addresses, transaction hashes, KYC documents, and payment trail.

Section 6. Bot checklist.
Ask: token type, operator/platform name, HTP resident status, user's role, whether activity is personal or business, mining or exchange details, transaction hashes, bank/payment evidence, tax year, and whether the issue is licensing, AML blocking, fraud, tax, accounting, or civil recovery.
